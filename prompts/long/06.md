Emergency drill routing
Case type: understanding_qna.

This fictional emergency-drill dossier tests understanding of a written procedure. No travel-time or distance calculation is required. The exercise is a tabletop rehearsal, and all directions below belong to its invented site.

CURRENT COMMAND SHEET
For a smoke report in the East Workshop, use Route Alder and gather at North Court. The Logistics Desk coordinates accessibility assistance. Communications during this drill use the radio channel. The site marshal approves changes to a route after an inspection; casual suggestions and historical maps cannot change the command sheet.

AMENDMENT RECORD
An initial command sheet said to use Route Birch and gather at East Court. The signed final amendment replaces both directions for East Workshop smoke reports. A later equipment memo discusses moving spare radios to the reception cupboard, but it does not replace the communications channel. The final command sheet printed above incorporates the signed amendment.

OBSERVED SCENARIO
The exercise facilitator announces smoke in the East Workshop. Route Alder is inspected and available. Route Birch is closed for maintenance. No site marshal instruction has superseded the current command sheet. A volunteer offers to lead the group to East Court because that is where earlier rehearsals met. A participant requests accessibility assistance and the Logistics Desk is available.

READING RULE
Choose the route and gathering place from the controlling sheet. Identify the team responsible for the assistance request and the stated communication channel. Do not interpret familiar meeting places as exceptions, infer a route from a compass direction, or estimate which path is faster.

SUPPORTING REGISTER
Reception notice: Reception staff distribute the current command sheet and direct drill questions to the facilitator; they cannot independently reopen a closed route.
Maintenance report: Route Birch is closed while stored materials are removed. A planned clearance has not been inspected or approved by the site marshal.
Access briefing: The Logistics Desk coordinates requested accessibility assistance with the marshal. Individual volunteers do not replace the designated coordination role.
Marshal log: The marshal inspected Route Alder and confirmed it was available for the scenario. The log contains no amendment returning the group to Birch.
Volunteer statement: A volunteer remembers East Court from an earlier rehearsal and recommends it. Memories are background observations rather than signed directions.
Radio equipment memo: Spare radios are now stored at reception. Their storage location changes neither the required communications method nor the gathering place.
Historical map: The old map highlights Birch and East Court. Its archive stamp explains why it conflicts with the controlling amendment for this drill.
Facilitator card: The scenario specifically names an East Workshop smoke report. Rules for a different workshop or a routine power test do not apply.
Visitor handout: Visitors are instructed to follow the current drill briefing. The handout does not offer a separate destination based on where they entered.
Assistance request: A participant asks for accessibility support. This activates the designated coordination team without changing the currently approved route or court.
Observation sheet: The facilitator records participants decisions for later review. Recording a suggestion does not approve the suggestion as an operational amendment.
Archive index: Earlier rehearsals remain filed for comparison. Archived sheets cannot supersede the current command sheet merely because they are easier to locate.

REVIEW NOTES
The word final on the signed amendment identifies its controlling status; the reader does not need to calculate document age. The amendment explicitly replaces the old route and court together. Mixing Alder with East Court would produce a direction that no current command sheet authorizes.

The accessibility request concerns who coordinates assistance. It does not announce a new smoke location or an unavailable route. The designated desk remains responsible even though a volunteer is willing to help. Route authority and assistance coordination are separate responsibilities in the briefing.

Reception is where spare equipment is stored and current sheets are distributed. It is neither the specified gathering place nor a substitute communications method. Answer the channel question from the command sheet, rather than from the location of the equipment used for that channel.

The closed Birch route stays closed until the marshal inspects and approves a change. A maintenance plan is a proposed future action. No approved reopening appears in the scenario, and the exercise does not ask participants to predict whether cleanup will finish.

The exercise facilitator records what participants decide but does not convert their suggestions into policy. The volunteer memory and old map should help explain the possible confusion while remaining subordinate to the signed direction for the announced East Workshop scenario.
