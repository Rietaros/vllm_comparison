Research consent withdrawal
Case type: understanding_qna.

This fictional research dossier concerns a participant's withdrawal request. It is a reading-comprehension exercise based only on the supplied protocol, rather than advice about an actual legal or clinical process. No age, duration, or retention-period calculation is required.

CONTROLLING CONSENT PROTOCOL
A participant may withdraw at any stage. On an authenticated withdrawal request, stop further collection and delete unsent audio. Retain the consent withdrawal record to document the request and its handling. The Ethics Coordinator owns the response. An assistant may acknowledge receipt but cannot refuse a withdrawal or replace the coordinator's decision. The protocol distinguishes research audio from the administrative withdrawal record.

PARTICIPANT CASE
Leila is an enrolled participant who gave confirmed consent through the approved form. She now sends an authenticated withdrawal request through the study's secure channel. Her interview audio remains unsent on the collection device. No data from that interview has been transmitted to the analysis team. The consent withdrawal record has been created, but the audio has not yet been removed. Leila asks the team to stop further interviews.

DOCUMENT PRIORITY
The signed protocol above controls over recruitment advertisements and draft assistant checklists. An old advertisement says participation is expected to continue until the study ends, but it does not limit the withdrawal right. A draft checklist recommends retaining every recording; that wording was rejected because it conflicts with the rule for unsent audio. The analysis team cannot make the participant continue simply because her interview would be useful.

SUPPORTING REGISTER
Enrollment record: Leila enrolled through the approved process and her initial consent was confirmed. Initial consent does not remove the later right to withdraw.
Withdrawal request: The secure-channel request is authenticated and explicitly asks the team to stop further interviews. No additional confirmation condition appears in the protocol.
Collection-device note: The interview audio is still on the device and has not been sent. It falls within the protocols explicit unsent-audio deletion instruction.
Analysis-team receipt: The analysis team confirms it has not received this interview. Its interest in the material cannot authorize transmission after authenticated withdrawal.
Assistant acknowledgement: An assistant acknowledges the request and forwards it to the Ethics Coordinator. That acknowledgement does not decide whether the request can be refused.
Coordinator responsibility: The Ethics Coordinator owns the response and ensures the stated handling steps are completed. Another team members preference does not transfer that responsibility.
Administrative record: The consent withdrawal record documents the authenticated request and subsequent handling. It is retained separately from the research audio that must be deleted.
Recruitment advertisement: The old advertisement describes the intended course of participation. The signed consent protocol controls where the advertisement suggests continuing after withdrawal.
Rejected checklist: A draft assistant checklist proposed retaining all recordings. Its rejected status prevents it from superseding the explicit unsent-audio instruction.
Scheduling note: A further interview was provisionally planned. An existing calendar entry does not permit further collection after the authenticated withdrawal request.
Researcher comment: A researcher says the interview would help the analysis. Scientific usefulness is not an exception to the withdrawal handling rule in this dossier.
Closure record: The coordinator records the response and confirms the requested handling. This administrative documentation is the retained record, rather than a substitute audio archive.

REVIEW NOTES
The protocol distinguishes initial enrollment from continued permission to collect material. Leilas confirmed consent explains why the interview began, and her authenticated withdrawal now activates the stated handling rule. The two facts do not contradict each other.

The audio remains unsent on the collection device. This condition identifies the protocol instruction that applies to it. There is no need to invent a rule for data already transmitted because the case explicitly says that no interview material reached the analysis team.

The retained consent withdrawal record is administrative evidence of the request and its handling. It is not the research audio. Keeping that record while deleting the unsent audio fulfills both instructions, rather than requiring the reader to choose one document-handling rule over the other.

An assistants acknowledgement confirms receipt and routes the request. The Ethics Coordinator remains the response owner. The protocol does not give the assistant a veto, require the analysis teams permission, or make a planned interview an exception to stopping collection.

The advertisement and rejected checklist explain possible procedural confusion but remain subordinate to the signed protocol. Intended participation and research usefulness cannot supersede the explicit withdrawal right. Return the requested handling fields rather than proposing a new exception.
