Heritage artifact provenance
Case type: understanding_qna.

A fictional archive is preparing a catalog entry for a carved wooden panel. The dossier contains donor claims, workshop records, ownership documents, and an approved catalog statement. Use the controlling statement to answer the questions; no date comparison or arithmetic is required.

APPROVED CATALOG STATEMENT
The panel was made by Anika at River Workshop. It is owned by City Archive. Approved image use is educational_only. These exact values are authorized by the catalog editor after reviewing the authenticated workshop mark and the signed transfer document. The panel's shipping crate is not evidence that the maker lived in the place where it was packed.

EVIDENCE PRIORITY
Authenticated workshop records and signed ownership transfers outrank unverified donor recollections. The final catalog statement replaces the preliminary label, which attributed the panel to Bima at Hill Studio. Retain the preliminary label in the research history, but do not repeat its attribution as the approved answer. A draft publicity note suggests unrestricted image use; the rights officer did not approve that suggestion.

CASE HISTORY
The donor supplied the panel with a crate marked Harbor Depot. A workshop specialist identified Anika's authenticated mark, matched it to River Workshop's register, and advised the catalog editor. A signed transfer gives ownership to City Archive. The rights officer permits educational image use only. The approved catalog statement above incorporates these findings and is the source for the public record.

Q&A RULE
Return the maker, workshop of origin, owner, and approved image-use value. Do not infer origin from the shipping crate, ownership from possession during restoration, or permission from an unapproved publicity draft. Use the exact approved spellings, including the underscore in the image-use value.

SUPPORTING REGISTER
Donor recollection: The donor remembers hearing Bimas name from a family member. No signed workshop document supports that memory, so it remains an unverified claim.
Workshop mark: A specialist authenticated the mark as Anikas and linked it to River Workshops accepted register. The final editor adopted this evidence.
Shipping crate: Harbor Depot is the packing and dispatch label. The crate identifies a shipping location and says nothing about the makers workshop.
Transfer document: The signed transfer names City Archive as owner. The restoration contractor received temporary custody, not a transfer of ownership.
Restoration receipt: The panel is being conserved at Stone Conservation. The receipt authorizes treatment and does not make the contractor the owner or maker.
Preliminary label: The preliminary label named Bima and Hill Studio before the mark was authenticated. It has been retained as superseded research material.
Rights decision: The rights officer approved educational_only use for images in this exercise. No signed approval authorizes unrestricted or commercial use.
Publicity draft: The publicity writer proposed unrestricted image sharing. The draft was returned for revision because it conflicted with the approved rights decision.
Catalog-editor approval: The editor signed the final statement after checking the workshop record and ownership transfer. It is the controlling catalog source.
Display agreement: A museum may display the panel on loan from City Archive. A loan changes the display location without changing the stated owner.
Research correspondence: A researcher asks whether Harbor Depot might be the origin. The archive replies that transport markings must be distinguished from authenticated maker evidence.
Archive history note: The original donor claim remains in the research file to document the investigation. Retention does not restore it as the approved attribution.

REVIEW NOTES
The maker and workshop fields come from the authenticated mark and accepted workshop register. A donor recollection may explain the preliminary label without supplying the final attribution. Keep that distinction when returning the authorized catalog fields.

Harbor Depot names a packing location. Stone Conservation names a temporary custodian, and the borrowing museum names a display location. These facts can all be true while River Workshop remains the origin and City Archive remains the owner.

The signed transfer governs ownership. Neither the donors earlier possession nor the contractors current custody overrides that transfer. The public catalog should therefore use the owner in the approved statement even if the panel is physically displayed elsewhere.

Image-use permission is the rights officers approved value, educational_only. The underscore is part of the required catalog value. A suggested publicity license remains unapproved and must not be blended into a broader permission that the officer never granted.

Retaining the superseded label gives researchers a record of the changed attribution. It does not turn the earlier maker into a second approved maker. The requested answer records the authorized fields, while the research history retains the explanation of why they changed.
