Scholarship award allocation
Case type: math.

A fictional scholarship committee allocates a fixed award budget. Every candidate receives a numerical score, but only eligible candidates enter the ranking. All amounts are whole credits, and all point totals are integers. Follow the supplied allocation policy without adding outside selection criteria.

FINAL ALLOCATION POLICY
The award budget is 1200 credits. Every selected candidate receives exactly 400 credits. Eligible means both application complete and recommendation approved. Candidate score equals academic points plus service points plus leadership points. Sort eligible candidates by descending score, then alphabetically by name when scores tie. Award candidates in that order while the remaining budget can fund a whole award. Do not split awards or skip a higher-ranked eligible candidate to fund a lower-ranked one.

CORRECTION RULE
The candidate name identifies its record. The last notice for a named field replaces that field; all other fields remain unchanged. A pending recommendation does not mean approved. An incomplete application is ineligible even if its points would be high. The register includes every submitted application so the committee can audit exclusions.

REVIEW NOTES
The allocation uses a fixed award amount rather than variable requests. A candidate cannot receive a smaller award to stretch the remaining budget. The committee wants a ranked award list and unspent balance under the final policy, not a proposal for a different distribution.

Bela has two academic-score corrections because the first amended transcription was still incomplete. Apply the second correction as the final academic field. Service and leadership points remain as registered. The notices never change her eligibility or create an additional candidate.

The alphabetical rule resolves equal total scores after eligibility filtering. It is not a preference used before scores are summed. Names are written in their final spelling in the register, and the exercise does not require locale-specific ordering beyond ordinary alphabetical comparison.

High scores among excluded applications should remain outside the award ranking. The policy requires a complete application and an approved recommendation together. Treating either condition as sufficient would change the awarded candidates and violate the supplied rule.

The budget is available in full at the start of allocation; there are no administrative charges or earlier commitments. The remaining amount is the original budget minus awarded credits. Report awardees in the actual ranking order, retaining the exact names from the register.
