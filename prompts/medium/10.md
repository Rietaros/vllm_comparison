Editorial correction workflow
Case type: understanding_qna.

A newsletter has discovered an incorrect place name in a published article. Use the editorial handbook below to decide its next step.

CURRENT HANDBOOK
A factual place-name error is handled by publishing a corrected page with a correction notice. The responsible owner is the section editor. Keep the original entry in the revision history. Quietly replacing the article without a notice is permitted for purely visual formatting adjustments, but not for factual corrections.

SOURCE RULE
For a factual correction, check the original interview transcript. An informal recollection in a chat is not enough to override the transcript. If the transcript is unclear, hold the correction for the section editor's review; do not invent a replacement place name.

CASE MATERIAL
The published article says the exhibition was at West Hall. The original interview transcript clearly says East Hall. The writer confirms that the place name was transcribed incorrectly. A chat participant thinks the article should be silently changed because the error is small. The design assistant can fix spacing but has no authority to approve a factual correction.

ARCHIVE NOTE
An old style memo advised deleting the original draft after any correction. It has been superseded by the current handbook's requirement to retain revision history. Neither the writer's confirmation nor the chat suggestion changes ownership of the correction. The transcript is available to the section editor.
